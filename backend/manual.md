# Operator manual

Synthetic manual for prototype use only.

## Pre-start walk around check
Before every shift, walk around the machine. Check tyres or tracks for damage, look for hydraulic oil leaks under the machine, and check that all lights, the horn, and the reversing alarm work. Test the service brake and the parking brake on level ground before moving. Check engine oil, hydraulic oil, and coolant levels with the machine parked level and the engine off. Clean mirrors and camera lenses. Do not start work if any brake or steering check fails. Report the defect instead.

## Seatbelt
The seatbelt must be fastened before the engine is started and must stay fastened whenever the machine moves or the attachments are operated. The rollover protective structure only protects an operator who is belted in. The system logs an incident whenever the seatbelt is open while the machine is moving.

## Speed limits
The site speed limit for the backhoe loader is 15 km/h. The excavator travel limit is 4 km/h. Reduce speed further on slopes, soft ground, and near people. Carry the bucket low while travelling.

## Slopes and rollover
Travel straight up or down slopes, never across them. Do not operate on a side slope steeper than 15 degrees. The system warns at 15 degrees of tilt and raises a critical alert at 20 degrees. If the machine starts to tip, lower the bucket to the ground and stay in the seat with the belt fastened. Never jump from a tipping machine.

## Proximity and blind spots
Keep everyone out of the swing radius. The rear and right side of the excavator are blind spots. Check mirrors and the camera before every swing and every reverse. If a person comes within 5 m, stop all movement and sound the horn. Only resume once they are clear and you have eye contact.

## Overhead power lines
Keep at least 3 m between any part of the machine and an overhead cable. Use a spotter whenever you work within 10 m of a line. If the boom contacts a line, stay in the cab, warn others to keep away, and wait for the power to be isolated.

## Drowsiness and fatigue
If you feel drowsy, stop the machine safely, lower the attachments, and tell your supervisor. The drowsiness monitor will slow and stop the machine automatically if the drowsiness signal crosses 0.70, sound a buzzer, and log an alert to the site safety authority. Acknowledge the alert only after you have taken a break.

## Hydraulic system and warning signs of pump wear
Normal hydraulic oil temperature is 55 to 80 C. Above 85 C, reduce the load and check the cooler for blockage. Rising vibration, a whining pump, oil pressure below 330 kPa, and slow cylinder movement are early signs of hydraulic pump wear. When the dashboard shows an elevated breakdown risk, finish the current cycle, avoid heavy digging, and raise a maintenance ticket for the hydraulic pump.

## Battery and charge
If the battery charge drops below 20 percent, avoid shutting the engine down away from the service area, as it may not restart. Report low charge with a maintenance ticket.

## Engine stalls
A stall usually means the bucket load is too high or the engine speed is too low. Lower the attachment, return controls to neutral, restart, and take a smaller bite. Repeated stalls should be reported.

## Trenching
Place spoil at least 1 m from the trench edge. Do not dig trenches in rain above 10 mm, as walls can collapse. Check walls for cracking after every pass. Nobody enters a trench deeper than 1.5 m without shoring.

## Truck loading
Load from the side or rear of the truck and never swing the bucket over the cab. Spread the load evenly and do not exceed the truck rating.

## Lifting and pipe laying
Only lift with the lift chart for the current boom position. Use certified slings and a tag line. Do not lift in wind above 30 km/h.

## Grading
Grading is not scheduled in rain above 5 mm because the surface cannot hold a level.

## Idling
Idle for no more than five minutes. If you will wait longer, lower the attachments and shut down. Idle time lowers the machine efficiency score.

## Machine efficiency score
The efficiency score is calculated per machine per day, from 0 to 100. It weights task completion rate at 35 percent, actual versus predicted time at 30 percent, idle time at 20 percent, and incident count at 15 percent. It measures the machine, not the operator.

## Reporting defects
Raise a maintenance ticket from any safety alert or from the Safety log screen. Tickets move from open to in progress to resolved. Do not operate a machine with an open brake or steering ticket.

## Shutdown
Park on firm level ground, lower all attachments, return controls to neutral, apply the parking brake, idle the engine for two minutes to cool the turbocharger, then shut down and remove the key.
